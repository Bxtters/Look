What research supports

Bone structure is mostly fixed. Appearance ratings still shift with sleep, skin health, grooming, expression, and body composition — factors studied in psychology and dermatology.

Sleep shows on your face

Observers rate well-rested faces as more attractive and healthier. Short sleep increases under-eye puffiness, dullness, and droop that read as fatigue within hours.

Axelsson et al., Royal Society Open Science / sleep & facial attractiveness literature

Skin clarity beats contour myths

Even skin tone and low inflammation are strong attractiveness cues across cultures. Daily broad-spectrum sunscreen, gentle cleansing, and treating acne/eczema with evidence-based care outperform unproven “face exercises.”

Dermatology consensus; Fink / Jones facial skin homogeneity studies

Smile and warmth score high

Genuine smiles raise attractiveness and approachability ratings more than a neutral face with “ideal” proportions. LOOK’s scorer already boosts mild smile blendshapes for this reason.

O’Doherty / social perception research on positive expression

Grooming is high leverage

A haircut that fits your face shape, managed brows, clean facial hair lines, and whitened/healthy teeth change ratings without changing bone. These are among the largest controllable deltas in appearance studies and consumer trials.

Grooming & dental aesthetics literature; dental smile attractiveness reviews

Body fat and fitness reshape the face

Lower body fat and higher fitness often sharpen jaw/cheek definition. Resistance training plus a sustainable calorie plan affects facial adiposity more reliably than viral “mewing” claims, which lack strong clinical evidence.

Body composition & facial adiposity; clinical skepticism of mewing RCTs

Skip smoke, cut heavy alcohol

Smoking accelerates skin aging (wrinkles, sallowness). Heavy alcohol dehydrates and inflames skin. Quitting is one of the best long-term face investments.

Dermatologic aging / tobacco literature

Routines that compound

Small daily habits beat one-off products. Adapt to your skin type and talk to a clinician before prescriptions (retinoids, acne meds).

  1. Morning — protect Rinse or gentle cleanse → moisturizer → SPF 30+ every day, including cloudy days. UV is the main preventable driver of visible skin aging.
  2. Night — repair Cleanse → treatment (adapalene/retinol if tolerated, or prescribed acne care) → moisturizer. Start slow to avoid irritation that worsens redness.
  3. Sleep — 7–9 hours Fixed wake time, dim screens late, cool dark room. Re-rate your LOOK score after a week of consistent sleep — fatigue drops fast when rest returns.
  4. Train — 3× strength / week Full-body lifts plus daily walking. Face definition often follows overall fat loss and posture more than isolated facial workouts.
  5. Groom — weekly reset Trim/shape facial hair, tidy brows, clean glasses frames, fresh haircut every 3–6 weeks. Keep nails and collar lines sharp — framing matters in real photos.
  6. Smile practice — natural, not forced In photos and first meetings, a relaxed Duchenne-style smile (eyes involved) consistently outperforms a blank stare in attractiveness ratings.

LOOK’s 1–10 score is a geometry toy from facial landmarks — not a medical or beauty diagnosis. Tips summarize general research themes; they are not personalized medical advice.
